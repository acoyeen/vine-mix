Before you install.

Will VineMix work on my PC?

VineMix is built for Windows 11 on x64 PCs. There are no native macOS, Linux or ARM builds. The app includes its .NET runtime, so you don’t need to install that separately.

Is VineMix free?

Yes. VineMix is free to download and use, with no subscription. Its source code is available under the MIT licence. Third-party virtual audio drivers have their own licences and may have separate costs for your use.

Do I need extra hardware or audio drivers?

You don’t need a hardware mixer. Start with a microphone and headphones available in Windows; this route needs no virtual audio driver.

To send your mix to another app as a microphone, you’ll need Virtual Audio Cable. VB-CABLE is a separate option for bringing audio in through a virtual input. Setup can guide you through installing missing drivers; this may require administrator permission and a restart.

Can I use it with voice chat, games or streaming apps?

VineMix can send your microphone and selected app audio together to a virtual microphone. Apps that let you choose a Windows recording device can receive that mix by selecting the cable’s recording device as their input.

Compatibility depends on the receiving app and your setup. Try the app’s microphone test or a short recording before a call or stream.

Do I need to know how to mix audio?

You can start simply: choose your microphone and headphones in the ready-connected starter. Both outputs begin muted, so you choose when to listen or share. Add effects and more audio sources when you need them.

Is my audio processed in the cloud?

No. VineMix mixes and processes audio on your PC. If you send your mix to a calling or streaming app, that app handles sharing it. VineMix can connect to GitHub to check for updates; automatic checks can be turned off in Settings.

Should I choose the installer or standalone version?

Choose the installer for its own app folder, shortcuts and guided setup for the drivers required for virtual inputs and outputs. Choose standalone if you prefer to launch a single EXE without installing the app; any virtual audio drivers are installed separately. Both use the same mixer and save settings on your PC.

The current beta is unsigned, so Windows may show an unknown publisher warning. Check the download against the checksum on its matching release page before deciding to run it.

Is the beta ready for everyday use?

It’s available to try, but bugs and compatibility issues are still possible. Long sessions, device recovery and latency still need further testing. Try your own setup before relying on it for an important call, recording or live stream.
